The Theater of Accountability: Power, Patronage, and the Fracturing of the Elite

Palanyag Editor•November 11, 2026

RAPPLER

The impeachment proceedings against Vice President Sara Duterte represent more than a mere legal hurdle; they are a profound symptom of the structural instability inherent in Philippine political life. While the halls of the House of Representatives echo with the high-stakes drama of accusations and rebuttals, the underlying reality is a seismic fracture in the architecture of the country’s recent power dynamics. This is not just a trial of an individual, but a public autopsy of an alliance that once promised a unified front, now decomposing under the weight of competing interests and the ruthless mechanics of dynastic survival.

In the Philippines, the line between personal vendetta and public service is often blurred by the 'trapo' culture, where the law is frequently wielded as a precision instrument of political realignment. The transition from the 'Uniteam' era to this current state of litigation reveals the fragility of elite pacts. When the machinery of the state is used to pursue an impeachment, the public must ask whether the primary objective is the restoration of institutional integrity or the strategic removal of a political rival. In a landscape where patronage remains the primary currency of governance, the impeachment process risks becoming a theater of 'epalan'—a performance of accountability designed to satisfy a restless electorate while merely shuffling the deck of the ruling class.

For the average Filipino, this political spectacle exists in a different spatial reality than the daily struggle for survival. While the elite engage in a high-budget chess game of constitutional maneuvers, the masses are forced into a perpetual state of 'diskarte'—the exhausting resourcefulness required to navigate a cost-of-living crisis, crumbling infrastructure, and a stagnant transport system. There is a profound disconnect between the grandiosity of the impeachment trial and the mundane, yet crushing, reality of a citizen trying to afford a meal or navigate a gridlocked city. The spectacle of power serves as a distraction from the systemic failures that define the lives of those outside the halls of authority.

Furthermore, this trial highlights the erosion of trust in our democratic institutions. When the mechanisms of accountability are perceived as partisan weapons, the sanctity of the office is diminished. If the impeachment process is seen as a tool for political liquidation rather than a genuine check on executive overreach, it deepens the cynicism of a public already weary of political theater. We must look beyond the headlines of the trial and demand a governance model that transcends the personalities involved. The goal should not be the mere removal of a figurehead, but the construction of a system where accountability is a constant, structural reality rather than a periodic, explosive event.

Ultimately, the impeachment of Vice President Duterte is a mirror reflecting the Philippines' ongoing struggle with its own political DNA. It exposes a nation caught between the desire for a clean break from the past and the gravitational pull of established power structures. To move forward, the nation must demand a political culture that prioritizes systemic reform over personal grievances. Until the focus shifts from the 'who' of the impeachment to the 'how' of governance, the Filipino people will remain spectators to a drama that rarely changes the script of their daily hardships.
