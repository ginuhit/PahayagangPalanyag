Northern Luzon Faces Widespread Displacement as Monsoon Rains Trigger Floods and Landslides

By Palanyag Reporter • November 25, 2026

ABS-CBN News

Thousands of residents across Northern Luzon are currently hunkered down in makeshift evacuation centers as torrential rains have triggered widespread flooding and landslides across several provinces. The National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC) confirmed on Wednesday that the combined effects of a recent tropical depression and the enhanced Southwest Monsoon have displaced at least 6,400 individuals across the Cagayan Valley and Cordillera regions.

In Cagayan, the rising waters of the Cagayan River have forced local government units to issue mandatory evacuation orders for low-lying barangays. Local authorities reported that several key provincial roads, including critical arteries connecting mountain towns to the lowlands, have become impassable due to mudslides. This has effectively isolated several farming communities from major supply hubs, complicating the delivery of essential goods and emergency medical services. According to preliminary assessments from the Department of Agriculture, the damage to rice and corn plantations is already estimated to be in the tens of millions of pesos. This represents a devastating blow to local farmers, many of whom were counting on a stable harvest to settle outstanding debts incurred during the planting season.

Rescue operations remain ongoing in the mountainous areas of Abra and Benguet. Local emergency responders, supported by the Philippine Coast Guard and various volunteer groups, have been working through the night to reach families stranded by sudden flash floods. While no fatalities have been officially confirmed in the last twelve hours, the NDRRMC has placed all affected areas under red alert status, urging residents to remain vigilant against the threat of secondary landslides. Relief goods, including canned food, rice, and hygiene kits, are currently being mobilized, though transport remains difficult due to deteriorating road conditions and the high risk of further soil movement.

The current weather pattern follows a series of intense low-pressure areas that have been passing through the Philippine Area of Responsibility (PAR) over the past two weeks. Meteorologists note that the intensification of the Southwest Monsoon, or Habagat, has significantly exacerbated the rainfall volume, making the flooding more unpredictable and severe than in previous months. This pattern of heavy, concentrated rainfall has raised significant concerns among environmental experts regarding the soil saturation levels in the northern highlands. The prolonged period of precipitation has reached a critical threshold, increasing the likelihood of catastrophic landslides if the rains persist.

For the communities affected, the struggle extends far beyond the immediate threat of rising water levels. Families in crowded evacuation centers are currently grappling with limited access to clean drinking water and a growing need for medical supplies to prevent the spread of waterborne diseases, such as leptospirosis and diarrhea. Small business owners in the affected towns also face significant economic losses as foot traffic disappears and local markets remain shuttered. As the weather bureau continues to monitor the movement of the next potential weather disturbance, the priority for local leaders remains the stabilization of food supplies and the swift restoration of electricity and communication lines to the most isolated provinces.
