Sandiganbayan Declares Three Co-Accused in P289-M Flood Control Scandal as Fugitives

By Palanyag Reporter • November 14, 2026

Rappler

The Sandiganbayan Fifth Division has officially declared three co-accused of dismissed Ako Bicol representative Zaldy Co as fugitives, marking a significant escalation in the legal battle over a controversial P289.4-million flood control project. In a ruling issued on Tuesday, November 10, the anti-graft court identified Sunwest board members Anthony Ngo and Consuelo Aldon, as well as Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) Mimaropa materials engineer Timogen Sacar, as individuals actively evading the law.

The court's decision comes after the trio failed to appear in court despite arrest warrants that were reportedly issued nearly a year ago. The Sandiganbayan emphasized that the accused have shown an “unmistakable intent to evade warrants and avoid prosecution.” In its resolution, the court observed that because of the high-profile nature of the corruption charges, it is reasonable to conclude that the accused are fully aware of the criminal proceedings against them. Their continued absence from the reach of law enforcement serves as a confirmation of a deliberate attempt to bypass the judicial process and obstruct the course of justice.

The charges involve graft and malversation related to the procurement and construction of a flood control system in Naujan, Oriental Mindoro. The project, intended to protect the municipality from seasonal flooding, was flagged for alleged irregularities and substandard construction. These discrepancies were first brought to public attention by Oriental Mindoro Governor Humerlito “Bonz” Dolor, whose findings regarding the defective infrastructure prompted the subsequent legal scrutiny. The investigation suggests that the failure to meet engineering standards may have resulted from compromised procurement processes.

To prevent the accused from escaping the country, the Sandiganbayan has ordered the immediate cancellation of the passports of Ngo, Aldon, and Sacar. The court noted that removing the official travel documents necessary for international movement is a critical step in hindering attempts to seek refuge overseas and ensuring the fugitives remain within the jurisdiction of the Philippine legal system.

The shadow of the case also falls heavily on Zaldy Co, the beneficial owner of Sunwest. Co remains at large and has reportedly fled the country to avoid the multiple cases filed against him. As a former representative of the Ako Bicol district, his involvement has added a layer of political complexity to the investigation. Sunwest has drawn intense scrutiny due to its prominence in national infrastructure; President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. previously identified the firm as one of the top flood control contractors under his administration. This connection highlights the potential for systemic issues when massive public works contracts are held by entities with close political ties.

For the people of Naujan and the province of Oriental Mindoro, this case is more than a matter of legal technicality. The alleged mismanagement of P289.4 million in public funds directly impacts the safety and security of local communities. In a nation increasingly vulnerable to the intensifying effects of climate change, flood control projects are not merely infrastructure—they are essential lifelines. When these projects are built with defects or through irregular procurement, the residents are left exposed to the devastating effects of typhoons and rising waters. The pursuit of these fugitives represents a crucial step in ensuring that public funds are used for their intended purpose: the protection and preservation of the Filipino people.
