DMW, UAE Authorities Target Freelance Visa Scammers; Pilot Digital Recruitment System in Dubai

By Palanyag Reporter • November 13, 2026

Rappler

The Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) has entered into close coordination with the United Arab Emirates’ Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratization (MOHRE) to track down Filipinos accused of orchestrating a massive freelance visa scam. This collaborative effort aims to dismantle a fraudulent scheme that has left hundreds of overseas Filipino workers (OFWs) in significant financial distress.

During a high-level visit to Dubai from November 7 to 8, DMW Secretary Hans Leo Cacdac confirmed that the agency is working hand-in-hand with host government officials to identify and prosecute those preying on the most vulnerable members of the Filipino community. The crackdown follows alarming reports that two Filipinos—prominent content creator Junie Sorsano and Ryan Ronel Baco—allegedly defrauded approximately 400 OFWs of AED 3.6 million, or roughly P62 million, through deceptive visa facilitation services.

The victims, who are primarily domestic helpers, caregivers, and retail sales staff, were targeted through the promise of freelance visas—a mechanism intended to allow for legal, self-sponsored employment. In reality, many of these workers were misled into believing the process was legitimate, only to lose significant portions of their hard-earned savings to these bad actors. The socio-economic impact on these families in the Philippines is profound, as the stolen funds often represent years of labor and sacrifice intended for their loved ones' futures.

In a strategic meeting with MOHRE head Dr. Abdulrahman Abdulmannan Al Awar, Cacdac emphasized that bilateral cooperation between the Philippines and the UAE is stronger than ever. He issued a stern warning to the suspects: should they attempt to flee the UAE to hide in the Philippines, the DMW is fully prepared to coordinate with the Department of Justice (DOJ) to pursue appropriate criminal charges. To mitigate the immediate suffering of those affected, the Secretary reminded OFWs that they can access legal assistance through the Migrant Workers Office (MWO) and may potentially tap into the agency's dedicated action fund.

Beyond the immediate pursuit of justice, the DMW is pivoting toward systemic prevention through aggressive digitalization. Cacdac signed a landmark agreement with the DataFlow Group to pilot the Digital Recruitment and Employer Accreditation Management (DREAM) System. This online platform is designed to allow UAE employers, principals, and recruitment agencies to submit documents and settle fees digitally, significantly cutting down the processing times and costs that often create gaps for middlemen to exploit.

This digital shift is set to transform the operational landscape of the MWO. By automating tedious clerical and documentation tasks, the agency aims to reallocate its personnel toward direct, high-impact community service. Cacdac explained that instead of being confined to desks, staff will be more mobile—visiting hospitals, prisons, and other areas where Filipinos congregate to provide hands-on legal and social support. This proactive approach complements the existing Online Employment Contract Verification System (OECVS), which has already successfully verified over 100,000 contracts since July.

The visit also served as a platform for broader diplomatic discussions, including the Philippines' upcoming role as chair of the Abu Dhabi Dialogue (ADD) in 2027. This forum, which includes several member states across the Middle East and Asia, is expected to focus heavily on the integration of Artificial Intelligence and digitalization in labor migration, signaling a future where technology serves as a shield for migrant rights.
