Human Rights Leaders Call for Collective Resistance Against AI-Driven Threats at Social Good Summit

By Palanyag Reporter • November 22, 2026

Rappler

Global human rights advocates and technology experts convened at Rappler’s Social Good Summit on Saturday, November 21, to address the escalating challenges posed by artificial intelligence (AI) and the urgent need for community-driven resistance against technology-driven threats. Under the theme 'We, The Humans,' the summit served as a platform for leaders to argue that human rights must remain the central priority in the rapid development of generative AI and other emerging technologies.

Amnesty International spokesperson Agnes Callamard issued a call to action, asserting that resistance against the profit-driven motives of the global AI race is not only possible but necessary. She emphasized that the public holds significant power when organized, suggesting that joining collective organizations is the most effective way to hold 'Big Tech' accountable. Callamard’s stance highlights a growing disparity: while technology evolves at an exponential rate, the legal and ethical frameworks meant to govern it often move at a glacial pace. This gap creates a vacuum where corporate interests can supersede the fundamental rights of individuals, particularly in the Global South. Her warning comes at a time when the influence of massive technology corporations often outweighs individual or even regional regulatory efforts.

The discussion also touched upon the personal and systemic risks of digital warfare. Nobel Laureate Maria Ressa shared insights from her own experience with online harassment, noting a disturbing trend in the Philippines. During the Duterte administration, Ressa and fellow panelist Agnes Callamard—a former UN special rapporteur—faced intense online attacks. Ressa revealed that in 2017, women in the Philippines were targeted by online harassment at a rate at least 10 times higher than that of men. This digital onslaught is not merely a matter of social discomfort but a direct assault on democratic processes and gender equality. Rather than succumbing to these attacks, Ressa explained that her team studied the data to develop systems that can help others defend against similar digital onslaughts, effectively turning the tools of harassment into a methodology for digital resilience.

As the summit progressed, the conversation shifted toward the intersection of AI and global conflict. Kristian Berg Harpviken, director of the Norwegian Nobel Institute, observed that AI is making modern warfare significantly more efficient. He noted that the current global landscape is defined by a struggle over where the reaches of AI will be permitted, highlighting a tension between technological capability and ethical boundaries. The integration of AI into military hardware introduces a terrifying new variable: the speed of decision-making. When algorithms dictate the parameters of engagement, the window for human intervention shrinks, raising profound questions about accountability and the laws of armed conflict.

Addressing the potential for global solutions, Jørgen Watne Frydnes, chairperson of the Norwegian Nobel Committee, suggested that breakthroughs in combating technology-driven threats will likely emerge from diverse coalitions. He argued that successful movements often require individuals to look beyond regional or local perspectives to engage in global cooperation. According to Frydnes, the ability of human beings to maintain cooperation despite the trauma of war and terror is a fundamental strength that can be leveraged to navigate the AI era. His call for global cooperation serves as a reminder that the digital frontier is borderless; a regulation passed in one nation can be easily bypassed by a server located in another, making the fight for ethical AI a truly transnational endeavor.

The summit concluded with a unified declaration: as technology continues to evolve, the preservation of human values and community integrity must remain the primary focus of global progress.
