Philippines to Host U19 Asian Sepaktakraw Championships; Local Youth Aim for Podium

By Palanyag Reporter • November 13, 2026

INQUIRER.net

The Philippines is poised to take center stage in the regional sporting arena as it hosts the Asian Sepaktakraw Federation (ASTF) U19 Asian Sepak Takraw Championships. Scheduled to run from November 26 to November 30, the tournament will be held at the historic Rizal Memorial Coliseum in Manila, bringing the continent's premier young athletes to the heart of the country.

For the home contingent, the mission is clear: secure podium finishes and assert dominance on home soil. Local standout players John Deryck Diego and Jay Ann Balwit have expressed profound confidence heading into the competition, fueled by the palpable momentum of the senior national team’s recent successes at the Asian Games. This recent high-level performance by the Philippine national squad has provided a significant psychological boost to the younger generation, effectively transforming the sport from a local pastime into a source of intense national pride and a legitimate pursuit of athletic excellence.

The selection of the Rizal Memorial Coliseum as the venue underscores the prestige of the event. As a landmark of Philippine athletic history, the coliseum provides a high-stakes environment that will test the mental fortitude of these U19 competitors. For many of these athletes, this championship represents their first major foray into the international spotlight, making the home-court advantage both a unique privilege and a heavy responsibility to perform under the gaze of a domestic audience.

Beyond the individual aspirations of players like Diego and Balwit, the hosting of this championship has significant implications for the local sporting community. It serves as a vital platform for showcasing the country's ability to organize and execute large-scale international sporting events with precision. Furthermore, the event provides a rare opportunity for local fans to witness elite-level sepak takraw without the need for international travel, a factor that may drive increased grassroots interest in the sport among students and youth across the provinces, potentially fostering a new wave of talent.

The presence of the Asian Sepaktakraw Federation in Manila also highlights the growing importance of youth development within the sport's global structure. By focusing on the U19 category, the federation is making a strategic investment in the long-term sustainability of sepak takraw, ensuring a steady pipeline of talent that can compete at the highest levels in the coming decade. For the Philippines, this is a crucial chance to solidify its reputation as a rising powerhouse in the discipline.

As the tournament approaches, the focus shifts to the intense preparation required for such a physically demanding sport. Sepak takraw demands a unique blend of acrobatic skill, lightning-fast reflexes, and surgical precision—qualities that these young athletes have been honing in anticipation of this moment. The eyes of the region will be on Manila this November to witness which nation's youth will rise to the top.
