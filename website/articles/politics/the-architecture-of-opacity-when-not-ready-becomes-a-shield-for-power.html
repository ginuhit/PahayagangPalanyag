The Architecture of Opacity: When 'Not Ready' Becomes a Shield for Power

By the Editorial Board•November 11, 2026

INQUIRER.net

In the theater of Philippine politics, the phrase 'not ready' is often less a statement of logistical delay and more a sophisticated maneuver of strategic silence. When Raffy Tulfo reports that a witness is 'not ready' to explain the earnings of GenCorp—a company linked to Vice President Sara Duterte—it highlights a recurring friction in our democracy: the deliberate construction of opacity by those who hold the highest seats of public trust. This is not merely a procedural hiccup in an investigation; it is a symptom of a systemic culture where the preservation of private interests often takes precedence over the public’s fundamental right to transparency.

At the heart of this issue lies the uncomfortable intersection of private wealth and public stewardship. For a high-ranking official, the management of corporate interests should ideally be a matter of impeccable disclosure to ensure that no conflict of interest poisons the well of public policy. However, when corporate earnings become a guarded secret, it fosters a climate of suspicion. It suggests a governance model where the 'family franchise' of power—a recurring theme in our local and national dynamics—extends into the boardroom, creating a gilded enclave of financial information that is inaccessible to the average taxpayer. This creates a two-tiered reality: one where the public navigates the daily grind of inflation and infrastructure decay, and another where the machinery of elite wealth operates behind a veil of 'unpreparedness.'

Furthermore, the use of 'not ready' as a rhetorical shield serves to exhaust the momentum of accountability. In a political landscape dominated by patronage and the slow-moving gears of bureaucracy, stalling is a potent weapon. By delaying the disclosure of financial ties, the subjects of scrutiny can wait for the news cycle to turn, for public outrage to cool, or for the political winds to shift. It is a form of 'diskarte' elevated to a state level—a survival instinct used by the powerful to navigate the scrutiny of the people. When the state’s investigative apparatus is met with such calculated hesitation, it risks signaling to the citizenry that the rules of transparency are negotiable, depending on how high one sits on the hierarchy of influence.

Ultimately, the integrity of our democratic institutions relies on the proactive pursuit of clarity, not the passive acceptance of excuses. The Filipino people, already weary of the systemic inequalities that define our urban and rural landscapes, deserve a government that views transparency as a prerequisite of office rather than a concession to be granted. If the earners of the nation’s highest offices cannot provide a clear accounting of their interests, they forfeit the moral authority to lead. We must move beyond the era of 'strategic silence' and demand a standard of disclosure that is as robust as the infrastructure we seek to build. Accountability cannot be postponed indefinitely; it must be made immediate, visible, and absolute.
