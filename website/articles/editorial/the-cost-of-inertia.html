The Cost of Inertia

By Palanyag Editor • November 13, 2026

INQUIRER.net

For many Cebuanos, the sound of heavy rain is no longer a soothing lullaby; it is a rhythmic, terrifying countdown to disaster. We have been conditioned to live with the constant, gnawing anxiety of rising waters, the dread of losing livelihoods, and the frantic, midnight scramble to move belongings to higher ground. This is not merely an inconvenience; it is a recurring trauma that haunts every monsoon season. But as the national government announces a massive ₱17 billion infrastructure injection for Cebu, we must ask a difficult, uncomfortable question: at what cost does this "delayed" protection come?

The numbers are staggering. Out of the ₱17 billion allocated for Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) projects, approximately ₱1.1 billion is earmarked for five water impounding structures across the Cotcot, Mananga, Butuanon, and Bulacao river systems. These river systems are the lifelines of our province, yet during heavy downpours, they become conduits of destruction. These proposed structures are not revolutionary concepts or sudden bursts of inspiration. They are merely fragments of the 2017 Cebu Flood Mitigation Master Plan—a blueprint that has sat on a shelf, gathering dust and being ignored by bureaucratic indifference, for nearly a decade.

It is easy to applaud the allocation of funds; it is much harder to confront the decade of inertia that preceded it. Department of Transportation Secretary Vivencio “Vince” Dizon noted that the 2017 plan was "unfortunately" not implemented. In the lexicon of governance, "unfortunately" is often a polite euphemism for systemic failure, shifting political priorities, or a profound lack of genuine urgency. While officials moved from one inspection to another, the residents of low-lying areas in Talisay and Cebu City were left to navigate the deluge alone. They paid the price for this inaction through damaged homes, lost commerce, and the constant, underlying fear that the next storm might be the one that takes everything.

We must look beyond the concrete and the steel. Infrastructure projects of this magnitude are more than just engineering feats; they are litmus tests for integrity. When billions of pesos are poured into river systems, the question is not merely whether the dams will hold, but whether the budget will hold its value. In a landscape where disaster is frequently used as a catalyst for sudden budget surges, we must demand absolute transparency. We need to ensure that these ₱17 billion are spent on long-term structural resilience rather than being swallowed by the inefficiencies, corruption, and leakages that often plague large-scale public works.

The 2027 budget promises a new era of flood control. But for the families living along the banks of the Mananga River, progress should not be measured in years of planning or billions in allocated funds, but in the absence of fear when the clouds darken. We do not need more "master plans" that exist only on paper; we need the accountability that ensures these structures actually stand as a bulwark between the citizens and the next typhoon.
